<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 5: 3D Design & Printing</h3> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">This week’s focus was on 3D design, printing, and scanning. Through these activities, I gained experience with Autodesk Fusion 360 for computer-aided design (CAD), Prusa Slicer software for preparing 3D printing files, and advanced 3D printing techniques. These tools and methods showcase the versatility of 3D printing, especially in creating parts with specific tolerances and designs that are difficult to produce with traditional tools or laser cutting.</p> <h5>Additive Manufacturing Project</h5> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">For this project, I designed a cube containing a free-moving sphere inside it. This design is particularly challenging using subtractive manufacturing because the sphere requires precise tolerances. It must be large enough not to fall out but small enough to move freely within the cube.</p> <img src="" alt="Fusion 360 model of the design"> <img src="" alt="Photograph of the printed object"> <h5>Preparing the Model for Printing</h5> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">After creating the CAD model in Autodesk Fusion 360, I exported it as a mesh and saved it as an STL file. I imported the STL file into Prusa Slicer, where I:</p> <ul style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light"> <li>Adjusted the infill settings.</li> <li>Added supports to ensure the object printed correctly.</li> </ul> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">Once the model was prepared, it was sent to the 3D printer. After printing, I carefully removed the support material using tweezers.</p> <img src="" alt="Final printed object with supports removed"> <h5>Printing Designs from Online Files</h5> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">In addition to creating my design, I explored pre-made 3D designs available online. The following objects were printed:</p> <ul style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light"> <li>Chelsea Desk Stand</li> <li>Articulating Dragon</li> <li>SD Card Case</li> </ul> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">These prints allowed me to better understand the potential of 3D printing for creating functional and decorative objects.</p> <img src="" alt="Photos of each printed object"> <h4>Scanning with LiDAR – RevoScan</h4> <h5>Attempting to Scan Objects</h5> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">I used a LiDAR scanner with RevoScan software for the 3D scanning portion of this assignment. Initially, I attempted to scan an IronMan flash drive, but the scanner struggled to detect the object. This issue was likely due to the object’s small size and shiny surface. Next, I attempted to scan a camera, but similar challenges arose with its reflective surfaces and detailed edges.</p> <img src="" alt="LiDAR scanner setup"> <img src="" alt="Photos of objects attempted to scan"> <h5>Challenges Encountered</h5> <ul style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light"> <li>The LiDAR scanner had difficulty detecting dark or shiny objects.</li> <li>Scanning fine details, such as corners, was problematic.</li> <li>Objects like the camera, calculator, and flash drive posed scanning challenges due to their material and shape.</li> </ul> <img src="" alt="Photos of issues during scanning"> <h5>Improvements for Future Attempts</h5> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light">For better results in the future, I would:</p> <ul style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light"> <li>Select objects with matte surfaces to reduce reflectivity.</li> <li>Choose simpler geometric shapes for easier scanning.</li> <li>Dedicate more time to refining the scanning setup and parameters.</li> </ul> </div>
