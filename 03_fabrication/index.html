<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 3: Hand Tools and Fabrication</h3> <h4>Kinetic Sculpture</h4> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;"> For my kinetic sculpture project, I drew inspiration from Beyblades, a spinning top toy that I frequently used when I was younger. </p> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;"> I began by creating a detailed drawing of the gears for the sculpture using AutoCAD. The gears were then laser cut along with a base plate, which was designed to house the circuitry for the kinetic sculpture. For the base plate, I precisely measured the distances between the centers of each gear to ensure proper alignment. </p> <div style="text-align: center;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\Beyblade_Piece_CAD.png" alt="AutoCAD file and laser cutting process" style="width: 30%; margin: 10px;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\Gear_Source.png" alt="AutoCAD file and laser cutting process" style="width: 30%; margin: 10px;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\Main_Box_Pieces.png" alt="AutoCAD file and laser cutting process" style="width: 40%; margin: 10px;"> </div> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;"> To power the kinetic sculpture, I referred to an example from class that utilized a simple DC motor controlled by an L9110 motor driver. The motor's rotational speed could be adjusted using a potentiometer. The example is available at the following link: </p> <p style="text-align: center;"> <a href="https://nathanmelenbrink.github.io/lab/arduino/L9110.html" target="_blank" style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;">L9110 Motor Driver Example</a> </p> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;"> Next, I assembled the kinetic sculpture. I used 5 mm diameter dowels to hold the gears in place and hot glue to attach spacers, ensuring that the gears could rotate freely on the laser-cut base plate. </p> <div style="text-align: center;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\Circuit_Assembled.JPEG" alt="Circuit and assembly process" style="width: 40%; margin: 10px;"> </div> <p style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light;"> The DC motor was attached to the center gear, providing power to the entire sculpture. The torque generated by the center gear transferred to the other gears, enabling the sculpture to move as a cohesive system. </p> <div style="text-align: center;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\Gears_attached.JPEG" alt="Circuit and assembly process" style="width: 40%; margin: 10px;"> </div> <div style="text-align: center;"> <img src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\IMG_2484.JPEG" alt="Completed kinetic sculpture" style="width: 40%; margin: 10px;"> </div> <div style="text-align: center; margin: 20px 0;"> <video controls style="width: 60%; max-width: 800px;"> <source src=".\Kinetic_Sculpture\IMG_2482.MP4" type="video/mp4"> Your browser does not support the video tag. </video> </div> <h4 style="text-align: center;">Challenges and Future Improvements</h4> <ul style="font: 15px Montserrat-Light; list-style-position: inside;"> <li><strong>Motor Axle Slippage:</strong> The DC motor axle occasionally slipped from the spacer because the fit was not secure enough. In future designs, I would create an asymmetric attachment to the DC motor to reduce the likelihood of slippage.</li> <li><strong>Durability of Materials:</strong> The gears, laser cut from thin wood, wore down over time. For improved durability, I would use thicker wood for the gears and construct the base plate from wood instead of cardboard. I would also secure the base with more permanent joints, such as screws or nuts and bolts.</li> </ul> </div>
